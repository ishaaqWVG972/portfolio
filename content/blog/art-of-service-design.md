Service design is the practice of designing, aligning, and optimizing an organization's operations to better support customer journeys. It goes beyond just the digital interface to consider every touchpoint a user has with a service.

## What Makes Service Design Different?

Traditional UX design often focuses on a single product or interface. Service design takes a holistic view, considering:

- **Frontstage interactions**: What the customer sees and experiences
- **Backstage processes**: The operations that make the experience possible
- **Support systems**: Technology, policies, and resources that enable the service

## Key Principles

### 1. User-Centered
Everything starts with understanding the people who use the service. This means going beyond personas to truly understanding context, emotions, and motivations.

### 2. Co-Creative
Service design is inherently collaborative. It brings together stakeholders from across an organization to design solutions that work for everyone.

### 3. Sequential
Services unfold over time. Understanding the journey—before, during, and after the core interaction—is essential.

### 4. Evidencing
Making the intangible tangible. Service evidence helps users understand and remember the value they received.

### 5. Holistic
Considering the entire environment in which a service exists, including the physical space, digital tools, and human interactions.

## Looking Forward

As our world becomes increasingly digital, the principles of service design become more important than ever. The best digital experiences are those designed with an understanding of the broader service ecosystem in which they exist.
