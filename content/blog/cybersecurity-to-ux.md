My career started in cybersecurity, analyzing threats, building defenses, and understanding how systems fail. It might seem like a strange path to UX design, but the skills are more transferable than you'd think.

## The Unexpected Connection

In cybersecurity, you spend a lot of time thinking about human behavior. Social engineering attacks work because they exploit how people think and act. Phishing emails succeed not because of technical sophistication, but because they understand human psychology.

This is exactly what UX design is about—understanding how people think and designing for real human behavior, not idealized behavior.

## What Security Taught Me About Design

### Assume Users Will Take Shortcuts
In security, we know users will choose convenience over security. They'll reuse passwords, click suspicious links, and skip warnings. Good security design works *with* these behaviors, not against them.

The same applies to UX. Users will take the path of least resistance. Design should make the right path the easy path.

### Small Friction Has Big Impact
A password complexity requirement adds a few seconds of friction. But across millions of users and daily logins, that friction compounds into real frustration and workarounds.

In UX, understanding the cumulative impact of small design decisions is crucial.

### Edge Cases Matter
Security thinking is all about edge cases. What happens if someone does something unexpected? What if the input is malformed? What if the network drops?

Robust UX design considers these scenarios too. The error state is as important as the happy path.

## Making the Transition

The shift from security to UX wasn't a complete career change—it was an evolution. I went from protecting users to empowering them. The core skill remains the same: understanding human behavior and designing systems that work for real people.

My security background gives me a unique perspective on UX. I think about privacy implications, potential misuse, and edge cases that others might overlook. It's made me a more thoughtful, thorough designer.
