Progressive disclosure is one of the most powerful patterns in UX design, yet it's often misunderstood or misapplied. Done right, it creates interfaces that are both simple for beginners and powerful for experts.

## What Is Progressive Disclosure?

Progressive disclosure is the practice of showing only the information or options users need at any given moment. Additional complexity is revealed as users progress or explicitly request it.

Think of it like layers in an onion—each layer reveals more depth, but you don't need to see all layers at once.

## Examples in the Wild

### Apple's Accessibility Settings
The main settings are straightforward. But tap "Accessibility" and you'll find dozens of sophisticated options for users who need them. These don't clutter the main interface.

### Google Search
Type a query, get results. But power users can use operators like `site:`, `filetype:`, and quotes for exact matches. The simple interface doesn't advertise these features, but they're there for those who know them.

### Figma's Properties Panel
Select an object and you see basic properties. But click into specific sections and you'll find advanced controls for masks, constraints, auto-layout, and more.

## Principles for Effective Progressive Disclosure

### 1. Default to Simple
The initial state should serve the majority of users. If 80% of users only need 20% of features, lead with those features.

### 2. Provide Clear Paths to Depth
Users who need advanced features should be able to find them. Use clear labels, intuitive grouping, and consistent patterns.

### 3. Remember User Preferences
If a user frequently uses advanced options, consider making them more accessible. Let the interface learn and adapt.

### 4. Don't Hide Critical Information
Progressive disclosure isn't about hiding things. Critical actions, warnings, and required fields should always be visible.

### 5. Maintain Context
When revealing additional options, don't disorient the user. The transition should be smooth and the relationship to the current task clear.

## The Balance

The art of progressive disclosure is finding the right balance. Hide too much and power users are frustrated. Reveal too much and new users are overwhelmed.

The best implementations feel natural—users don't think about it. They find what they need when they need it, and they're never lost in complexity they don't require.
